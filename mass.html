The Mass

The Catechism calls the Eucharist the source and summit of the Christian life (CCC 1324). At Mass we listen to God's word and we receive Jesus himself in Holy Communion. The Church asks Catholics to take part every Sunday and on holy days of obligation (CCC 2180-2183).

The parts of the Mass

The Mass has four main parts. The Catechism describes its basic structure at CCC 1345-1355.

1. Introductory Rites

We gather, sing, and ask God's mercy. This is a time to settle your heart and leave the week behind.

2. Liturgy of the Word

We hear readings from Scripture, including the Gospel, then a homily, the Creed, and prayers for the Church and the world.

3. Liturgy of the Eucharist

Bread and wine are brought forward and become the Body and Blood of Christ. We pray the Our Father and receive Communion.

4. Concluding Rites

We receive a blessing and are sent out to live what we celebrated and to share God's love.

What we believe about the Eucharist

Catholics believe that Jesus is truly present in the Eucharist, body, blood, soul, and divinity (CCC 1373-1377). This is why the Church asks us to prepare before receiving Communion, including fasting for one hour beforehand and being free of serious sin (CCC 1385-1387). If you are aware of a serious sin, go to confession first.

How to take part more fully

  • Read the day's readings before Mass at bible.usccb.org.
  • Arrive a few minutes early and spend a moment in quiet prayer.
  • Join in the responses and the singing, not just listen.
  • Listen to the homily and ask yourself what one thing God is saying to you.
  • After Communion, stay a moment in thanksgiving.
  • Take one small thing from the homily into your week.

Go deeper

Read the Catechism's teaching on the Eucharist (Part Two, Section Two, Article 3) at usccb.org and ask your parish priest any questions you have about the Mass.
