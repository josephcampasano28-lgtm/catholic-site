The Seven Sacraments

The Catechism teaches that the sacraments are visible signs, given by Christ to the Church, that give us his grace (CCC 1113-1131). There are seven, and they touch the most important moments of our lives (CCC 1210-1211). They fall into three groups.

Sacraments of Initiation

These bring us into the life of the Church.

Baptism

Frees us from sin and makes us children of God and members of the Church. It is the doorway to all the other sacraments (CCC 1213-1284).

Confirmation

Strengthens us with the Holy Spirit and completes the grace of Baptism, so we can live and witness to our faith (CCC 1285-1321).

Eucharist

The body and blood of Christ, the source and summit of the Christian life. Catholics receive Jesus himself at Mass (CCC 1322-1419).

Sacraments of Healing

These bring Christ's mercy and healing when we are weak.

Penance and Reconciliation

Confession. We admit our sins, receive God's forgiveness through the priest, and are reconciled with God and the Church (CCC 1420-1498).

Anointing of the Sick

Gives strength, peace, and courage to those who are seriously ill or growing old, uniting their suffering to Christ (CCC 1499-1532).

Sacraments at the Service of Communion

These are directed toward the salvation of others.

Holy Orders

Through this sacrament, men are ordained as bishops, priests, and deacons to serve the Church (CCC 1536-1600).

Matrimony

A man and a woman make a lifelong covenant of love, open to children, and receive grace to live it out (CCC 1601-1666).

How to live the sacraments

  • Go to Mass every Sunday and on holy days, and prepare by reading the readings beforehand.
  • Make confession a regular habit. Many Catholics go monthly.
  • If you are not yet baptized or confirmed, talk to your parish about RCIA or confirmation preparation.
  • Find your parish's confession and Mass times on its website or bulletin.
