Commandments and Beatitudes

The Catechism teaches that the commandments are not a list of rules to fear. They show us how to live in love of God and neighbor (CCC 2052-2074). The Beatitudes, which Jesus gave in the Sermon on the Mount, show us what a happy life with God looks like (CCC 1716-1729).

The two greatest commandments

Jesus summed up the whole law in two commands: love God with all your heart, soul, and mind, and love your neighbor as yourself (Matthew 22:37-40). The Ten Commandments unfold these two. The first three are about loving God, and the last seven are about loving others (CCC 2055).

The Ten Commandments

Here is each commandment in plain words, with the Catechism section that explains it.

1. Put God first

Worship God alone. Don't let money, fame, or pleasure take the place that belongs to him. (CCC 2083-2141)

2. Respect God's name

Speak of God and holy things with reverence, never carelessly or as a curse. Keep your promises. (CCC 2142-2167)

3. Keep the Lord's Day holy

Go to Mass on Sundays and holy days, and set aside time for rest and family. (CCC 2168-2195)

4. Honor your father and mother

Respect and thank your parents and those in rightful authority. Parents in turn care for their children. (CCC 2196-2257)

5. You shall not kill

Human life is sacred from conception to natural death. This also calls us to fight anger and hatred. (CCC 2258-2330)

6. You shall not commit adultery

Be faithful and pure, and respect the gift of love and sexuality as God designed it. (CCC 2331-2400)

7. You shall not steal

Respect other people's property, pay fair wages, and care for the poor. (CCC 2401-2463)

8. You shall not bear false witness

Tell the truth. Avoid lies, gossip, and judging others unfairly. (CCC 2464-2513)

9. Purity of heart

Guard your thoughts and desires, and treat others with respect in your heart as well as in your actions. (CCC 2514-2533)

10. Don't envy

Be free from greed and jealousy, and trust that God provides what you need. (CCC 2534-2557)

The Beatitudes

In Matthew 5:3-12, Jesus names the kinds of people who are blessed. Here they are in short form, with what each one asks of us (CCC 1716-1729).

Poor in spirit

Depend on God, not on your own strength or possessions.

Those who mourn

God comforts those who grieve and cares about our pain.

The meek

Be gentle and humble instead of pushing for your own way.

Hunger for righteousness

Want what is right and good more than you want comfort.

The merciful

Forgive and help others, and you'll receive mercy too.

The pure in heart

Keep your heart focused on God, and you will see him.

The peacemakers

Work to bring peace, and you'll be called children of God.

The persecuted

Staying faithful to Christ is worth it, even when it costs something.

How to live them this week

  • Each evening, look back on your day and ask which commandment you kept well and which you struggled with. This is called an examination of conscience.
  • Pick one Beatitude each week to practice, like showing mercy or making peace.
  • Take anything you struggled with to confession.
  • Read Matthew 5 slowly, one verse at a time.
