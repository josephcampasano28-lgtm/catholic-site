Prayer

Prayer is how we talk with God and listen to him. It isn't only for church or for special moments. The Catechism teaches that prayer is a living relationship with God (see CCC 2558-2565), and that we are meant to pray throughout our daily lives (CCC 2742-2745).

Three ways to pray

The Catechism describes three main expressions of prayer (CCC 2700-2719):

  • Vocal prayer: praying with words, like the Our Father or the Hail Mary.
  • Meditation: quietly thinking about Scripture or the life of Jesus and what God is saying to you.
  • Contemplative prayer: simply being in God's presence, often in silence.

Why we pray

The Catechism names five main reasons for prayer (CCC 2626-2643): blessing and adoration, petition (asking for what we need), intercession (praying for others), thanksgiving, and praise.

Prayers to learn

Sign of the Cross

In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Our Father

Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come; thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread; and forgive us our trespasses as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

The Catechism's long reflection on this prayer is at CCC 2759-2865.

Hail Mary

Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee; blessed art thou amongst women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

Glory Be

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

Simple ways to start a habit

  • Pick one set time each day, like morning or bedtime, and keep it short at first.
  • Read the day's Mass readings at bible.usccb.org.
  • Pray a decade of the Rosary, which is one Our Father, ten Hail Marys, and one Glory Be.
  • End each day by thanking God for something specific.

Go deeper

Read the Catechism's full section on prayer (Part Four) at usccb.org and talk with your parish priest about building your prayer life.
