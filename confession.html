Confession Guide

Confession, also called Reconciliation or Penance, is the sacrament where God forgives our sins through the priest. Jesus gave his apostles the power to forgive sins (John 20:21-23), and the Catechism explains the sacrament at CCC 1420-1498. Everything you say is completely private. A priest may never reveal what is said in confession (CCC 1467).

Everyone goes through the same basic steps, but what helps most depends on your age. Choose your guide on the side.

Guide for Kids

Confession is a chance to tell God you're sorry and to hear that he forgives you. The priest is there to help, not to scare you. You can't do it wrong, and God is happy you came.

What happens, step by step

  1. Wait and pray. Ask God to help you remember and to be brave.
  2. Begin. Make the Sign of the Cross and say: "Bless me, Father, for I have sinned. It has been ___ since my last confession." If it's your first time, say "This is my first confession."
  3. Tell your sins. Say the things you did wrong. If you forget something or get nervous, the priest will help you.
  4. Listen. The priest will give you advice and a penance, which is usually a prayer or a kind thing to do.
  5. Say you're sorry. Pray an Act of Contrition.
  6. Be forgiven. The priest says the words of forgiveness. Say "Amen," then "Thank you, Father," and do your penance.

A simple Act of Contrition

My God, I am sorry for my sins. I love you and I want to do better. Please forgive me and help me to be good. Amen.

Your parish or teacher may teach you a specific version. Use that one if they do.

Helping you remember

Think about these questions quietly. You don't need to have a long list. One or two things is just fine.

With God

  • Did I forget to pray?
  • Was I disrespectful at Mass?
  • Did I use God's name carelessly?

With my family

  • Did I obey my parents?
  • Did I talk back or throw a fit?
  • Was I mean to my brothers or sisters?

With others

  • Did I lie or cheat?
  • Did I take something that wasn't mine?
  • Was I unkind, or did I leave someone out?

With myself

  • Was I selfish or did I refuse to share?
  • Did I waste time instead of doing homework or chores?

Important: If anyone has hurt you or made you feel unsafe, tell your parents or another adult you trust right away. It is never your fault.

For parents: Practice the steps at home, pray together beforehand, and go with your child. Ask your parish about its preparation for first confession.

Guide for Teens

Confession is for real life, and the priest has heard it all. He's there to help you, not to judge you. It's private, and it is one of the best ways to reset when things feel heavy.

What happens, step by step

  1. Prepare. Take a few quiet minutes to pray and think back over your life since your last confession.
  2. Begin. Make the Sign of the Cross and say: "Bless me, Father, for I have sinned. It has been ___ since my last confession."
  3. Confess. Tell your sins honestly. For serious sins, say what it was and about how often (CCC 1456). If you're nervous, it's fine to say so.
  4. Listen. The priest may give advice and will give you a penance.
  5. Act of Contrition. Pray it, in your own words or a traditional form.
  6. Absolution. The priest forgives you in God's name. Then do your penance.

A traditional Act of Contrition

O my God, I am heartily sorry for having offended Thee, and I detest all my sins because of Thy just punishments, but most of all because they offend Thee, my God, who art all good and deserving of all my love. I firmly resolve, with the help of Thy grace, to sin no more and to avoid the near occasion of sin. Amen.

Looking honestly at your life

Faith

  • Did I skip Mass or prayer without a good reason?
  • Was I embarrassed to be Catholic?
  • Did I treat holy things as a joke?

Family

  • Did I disrespect or lie to my parents?
  • Did I ignore rules I knew were reasonable?
  • Was I cruel to siblings?

Friends and school

  • Did I cheat, lie, or gossip?
  • Did I bully, tease, or leave someone out?
  • Did I give in to peer pressure?

Phone and online

  • Did I say or post cruel things?
  • Did I look at, send, or share things that disrespect me or others?
  • Did screens take over my time, sleep, or prayer?

Choices and body

  • Did I misuse alcohol or drugs?
  • Did I treat my body and other people with respect?
  • Did I pressure anyone to do something they didn't want to?

Tips

  • Not sure if something is serious? Say it and ask the priest.
  • Nervous about face to face? Many parishes offer a screen.
  • You can ask questions about faith or life in confession too.
  • Doubts and questions about faith aren't sins. Bring them up.

If things feel heavy: If you're struggling with depression, anxiety, or thoughts of hurting yourself, please talk to a parent, school counselor, or another trusted adult. In the US you can call or text 988 any time. If someone is hurting or abusing you, tell a trusted adult right away.

Guide for Adults

Whether you go often or haven't been in years, the door is open. The Catechism teaches that mercy is always available (CCC 1422, 1446). The Church asks Catholics to confess serious sins at least once a year (CCC 2042), and before receiving Communion if they are aware of serious sin (CCC 1385).

What happens, step by step

  1. Examine your conscience. Pray and review your life since your last confession.
  2. Be sorry. Contrition, real sorrow and the desire to change, is the most important part (CCC 1451-1454).
  3. Begin. Sign of the Cross, then: "Bless me, Father, for I have sinned. It has been ___ since my last confession."
  4. Confess. Name your sins simply and honestly. Serious sins must be confessed by kind and number (CCC 1456). Confessing lesser sins is also encouraged (CCC 1458).
  5. Penance. The priest gives advice and a penance to help repair and heal (CCC 1459-1460).
  6. Act of Contrition and absolution. Pray the Act of Contrition while the priest absolves you.
  7. Give thanks. Do your penance and thank God.

A traditional Act of Contrition

O my God, I am heartily sorry for having offended Thee, and I detest all my sins because of Thy just punishments, but most of all because they offend Thee, my God, who art all good and deserving of all my love. I firmly resolve, with the help of Thy grace, to sin no more and to avoid the near occasion of sin. Amen.

Examination of conscience

This follows the Ten Commandments. See the Commandments page for more.

God first (1st to 3rd)

  • Have I made prayer a priority?
  • Have I put money, work, or pleasure before God?
  • Did I miss Sunday Mass without good reason?
  • Have I used God's name carelessly?

Family and duty (4th)

  • Have I been patient with my family?
  • Have I neglected my duties at home or work?
  • Have I honored my parents and cared for them?

Anger and life (5th)

  • Have I held grudges or refused to forgive?
  • Have I been harsh in words or actions?
  • Have I harmed my health through abuse of alcohol or drugs?

Faithfulness and purity (6th, 9th)

  • Have I been faithful in thought and action?
  • Have I guarded my eyes and mind, including online?
  • Have I respected others as persons, not objects?

Honesty and money (7th, 8th)

  • Have I been honest at work and with money?
  • Have I been generous to those in need?
  • Have I lied, gossiped, or damaged someone's reputation?

Contentment (10th)

  • Have I been envious or greedy?
  • Have I trusted God to provide?
  • Have I failed to do good I could have done?

Tips

  • It's been a long time? Just say so. The priest will guide you and there's no need to be afraid.
  • You can't say it wrong. If you forget something, it's still forgiven.
  • Call your parish office to find confession times or to make an appointment.
  • Consider going regularly. Many Catholics go monthly.
